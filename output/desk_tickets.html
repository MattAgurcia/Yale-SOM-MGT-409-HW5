What would have been simpler as one agent with tools, and why?

Most of it. For three tickets, four products, three vendors and one lease, one operations agent with the same tools would have been cheaper and easier to debug.

  • The Boss used 318,958 of the run's 635,625 tokens (50%), mostly re-reading instructions and reports.
  • About 97% of all tokens were input. Every hand-off means another agent reads its prompt and the ticket again (get_ticket was 26 of 67 tool calls).
  • The 101 and 102 closes, where the Boss worked alone, took 4 model calls each. 103's first close used three hand-offs and 141,644 tokens to file one $0 price request and write one draft.
  • The safety lives in the MCP server (human-only approvals, no negative cash, no approving a purchase order while the vendor has an unpaid invoice), so one agent would hit the same walls.

The one hand-off I'd keep is Customer Service. A dedicated writer kept invoice 501 and the cash problem out of every customer letter. Five agents make sense when the shop has genuinely separate jobs (a bookkeeper, a buyer, a front desk) or enough volume to work in parallel. Here the specialists worked one after another, so there was no speed gain either.

Three new problems this team could solve with the tools I built

1. One inbox for every dollar. I first guessed the agents could approve expenses automatically. They can't, and the shop shouldn't want that, since a human approves every payment by design. What they can do is put every request in one place with the amount, the reason and what checking would hold afterward. The October rent (lease 1 is due 2026-10-02) is the obvious next case. Once someone files it, the board would show it $2,248.00 short, next to every other bill competing for the same $152.00.

2. Bulk quotes for student groups. Customer Service already combined my approved price with Inventory's stock count in draft 4. A club asking for 30 Yale Caps (40 on hand, $6 cost, $22 list) is ticket 103 without the hard parts. There's no shortfall and no vendor block, just a price at or above cost that I approve and a quote that names only that price. (Facilities adds little here, since it only knows the lease.)
